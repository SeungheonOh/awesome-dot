# Recipe Card Collection

Design a consistent recipe-card collection that preserves supplied quantities and supports easy kitchen reading.

## Scenario

Friends have contributed recipes for a small private collection, but the submissions use different layouts and measurement styles. You want attractive cards that are easy to read with messy hands nearby. The design should preserve what contributors actually supplied and make missing cooking details obvious.

## Inputs to prepare

- Approved recipe text with contributor permission and credits
- Authorized food images or a text-only preference
- Card dimensions, printing constraints, and intended recipients
- Measurement conventions and user-confirmed dietary or allergen wording

## Copy this prompt into dot

```text
dot, design a recipe-card collection from [APPROVED RECIPES] for [PRIVATE RECIPIENT GROUP]. Use [CARD DIMENSIONS], [PRINT CONSTRAINTS], and [AUTHORIZED IMAGES OR TEXT-ONLY CHOICE]. First inventory titles, servings, ingredients, steps, timing, and contributor credits. Flag missing quantities, inconsistent units, or unclear stages rather than inventing cooking instructions. Keep any dietary or allergen wording limited to [USER-CONFIRMED WORDING]; do not certify a recipe as safe for a dietary need.

Propose a simple card system with a clear title, serving count, ingredient list, numbered method, and optional notes area. Preserve the supplied quantities and meaning. Ask before converting measurements or changing ingredient order in a way that could confuse the method. Use separately editable text, readable type, and generous spacing. If a recipe needs two sides or a second card, show the continuation explicitly instead of shrinking it excessively.

Deliver editable card templates and review exports if available, or a complete layout specification, plus a recipe-to-card checklist. Test a very short recipe, a long ingredient list, monochrome printing, and a recipe with no photo. Verify quantities and step order against the source after layout. Do not distribute the collection, upload family recipes, or make unsupported nutrition claims. Ask me to approve final content, contributor credits, and recipient scope before sharing.
```

## Iterate with a purpose

### 1. Check recipe fidelity

```text
Compare every card’s quantities, servings, and step order with its approved source and list any unresolved mismatch.
```

### 2. Build an index

```text
Create a separately editable index using supplied recipe categories and contributor names, without inferring dietary suitability.
```

### 3. Prepare kitchen format

```text
Adapt the approved cards into a larger kitchen-reading format while retaining recipe identifiers and explicit continuation labels.
```

## Expected deliverables

- Reusable recipe-card template
- Laid-out review cards if supported
- Source-fidelity and missing-detail checklist
- Contributor credit and recipient-scope record

## Acceptance checks

- All quantities and serving counts match the approved source
- A long recipe continues clearly rather than becoming unreadably small
- Photo-free and monochrome versions remain usable
- Unconfirmed allergen or dietary claims are absent
- Missing timings and ambiguous instructions remain flagged
- Contributor credits are preserved

## Access, privacy and stop conditions

- The layout project does not validate cooking safety or nutrition
- Only authorized recipes and photographs may be included
- Document and print exports depend on available tools
- Distribution and third-party uploads require recipient-specific approval

## Two possible extensions

- Create a matching divider and index system
- Collect contributor corrections before making a second edition
