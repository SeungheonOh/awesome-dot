# Volunteer Handbook Design

Transform approved volunteer guidance into a navigable handbook with clear escalation and revision cues.

## Scenario

A community organization’s volunteer instructions have grown into a long, uneven document. New volunteers struggle to find arrival details, role expectations, and the right contact when something is unclear. You want a friendly handbook that improves navigation while preserving approved policies and avoiding sensitive personal information.

## Inputs to prepare

- Approved handbook text and the organization’s authoritative policy references
- Volunteer roles, frequent first-day questions, and intended audience
- Role-based contact information approved for inclusion
- Format, brand guidance, accessibility priorities, and revision owner

## Copy this prompt into dot

```text
dot, redesign [APPROVED VOLUNTEER HANDBOOK TEXT] for [VOLUNTEER ROLES] and [INTENDED AUDIENCE]. Use [AUTHORITATIVE POLICY REFERENCES] as the source of truth. Begin with a content inventory and a proposed structure organized around [FIRST-DAY QUESTIONS]. Flag contradictions, missing responsibilities, and outdated contact details; do not invent policies, legal requirements, or personal commitments on the organization’s behalf.

Create a welcoming but practical hierarchy with arrival information, role-specific tasks, common questions, and clear stop-and-ask guidance. Preserve approved policy wording where exact language matters and distinguish summaries from the full policy. Use only [APPROVED ROLE-BASED CONTACTS]; exclude private phone numbers, participant records, and unnecessary personal details. Add a revision date and owner-review panel. Keep text editable and make headings, links, and navigation useful for both screen and print.

Deliver an editable handbook or full layout specification, a review export if supported, a source-change log, and a five-question navigation test. Check a volunteer with more than one role, a missing contact, a long policy section, and grayscale printing. Verify bookmarks, reading order, and links where supported; disclose untested accessibility features. Keep all drafts private and ask for approval of final policies, recipients, and distribution method before uploading or sending the handbook.
```

## Iterate with a purpose

### 1. Test the first day

```text
Use five realistic newcomer questions to test the draft and record the exact heading or page where each answer is found.
```

### 2. Prepare role extracts

```text
Create separate proposed quick-reference extracts for [ROLES], keeping shared policies consistent and linking back to the approved handbook.
```

### 3. Plan the next revision

```text
Build an owner-review checklist for contacts, policies, dates, and role changes, including a method to identify obsolete printed copies.
```

## Expected deliverables

- Reorganized handbook content and layout
- Editable source and review export if supported
- Source-change and unresolved-policy log
- Navigation and accessibility test notes
- Revision-owner and distribution checklist

## Acceptance checks

- Policy summaries are distinguishable from authoritative wording
- Role-based contacts are approved and current or flagged
- A missing contact produces stop-and-ask guidance
- Multi-role volunteers can locate all applicable sections
- Private participant details are excluded
- Links and exported navigation are tested or marked unverified

## Access, privacy and stop conditions

- The redesign does not create or interpret binding organizational policy
- Private records and unapproved personal contact details are excluded
- Document and accessibility features depend on available tools
- Uploading or distributing the handbook requires approval of files and recipients

## Two possible extensions

- Prepare an approved first-day orientation handout
- Create a review form for volunteers to report confusing sections
