# Small-Press Zine Layout

Lay out a short zine with a coherent reading sequence and verifiable folded-page order.

## Scenario

Your team wants a small printed zine about lessons from a learning week, using short essays and authorized sketches. Contributors have different writing styles, and the booklet must work on an ordinary office printer. You need a reviewable layout that keeps unapproved project details out.

## Inputs to prepare

- Approved short essays, captions, and authorized illustrations
- Target reader, tone, and explicit internal or public visibility
- Page count, paper size, folding method, and printer limitations
- Contributor credits, confidentiality exclusions, and accessibility needs

## Copy this prompt into dot

```text
dot, design a short zine about [TOPIC] from [APPROVED TEXT AND AUTHORIZED IMAGES] for [READERS]. The permitted visibility is [INTERNAL OR PUBLIC SCOPE], and [EXCLUDED DETAILS] must not appear. Start with a page-by-page reading plan that states where each contribution fits. Ask before cutting an argument, changing a contributor’s meaning, or adding unsupported facts. Mark missing credits or permissions rather than inventing them.

Use [PAGE COUNT], [PAPER SIZE], and [FOLDING METHOD]. Propose a simple type hierarchy, generous margins, and an image treatment that works on [PRINTER LIMITATIONS]. Keep the reading-order layout separate from any folded-print arrangement. If layout tools support the requested format, produce editable source and two clearly labeled PDFs: reading order and print arrangement. Otherwise provide an exact assembly specification and disclose the limitation.

Deliver a content map, caption and credit register, export notes, and a short folding test. Verify page order with numbered placeholder pages before relying on the real layout. Check inner margins, image rights notes, a very short contribution, an overlong paragraph, and black-and-white legibility. Report whether reading order and accessibility were tested in the exported PDF. Do not publish, circulate, or send the zine to a printer without approval of the final audience and files.
```

## Iterate with a purpose

### 1. Test the paper dummy

```text
Make a numbered paper-dummy plan for the chosen fold and verify which pages become the cover, center, and back before final export.
```

### 2. Balance contributions

```text
Review the approved layout for uneven contributor prominence and propose cuts or spacing changes for approval without rewriting their opinions.
```

### 3. Create an accessible companion

```text
Prepare a linear text companion with headings, captions, image descriptions, and credits so the content can be read without the folded layout.
```

## Expected deliverables

- Page-by-page editorial map
- Editable zine source if supported
- Separately labeled reading and print-order candidates
- Caption and credit register
- Fold test and export notes

## Acceptance checks

- Every approved contribution appears in the content map
- Print order is checked with a numbered dummy
- An overlong paragraph does not disappear or clip
- Inner margins allow for the chosen fold
- Image and contributor credits match supplied permissions
- Exported accessibility limits are disclosed

## Access, privacy and stop conditions

- Booklet layout and PDF features vary by available software
- Print arrangement must be verified against the actual folding and printer setup
- Contributor meaning and unpublished material require careful approval
- No publication, circulation, or print order is authorized

## Two possible extensions

- Prepare a low-bandwidth web-readable companion
- Adapt the approved grid for a second issue without reusing stale facts
