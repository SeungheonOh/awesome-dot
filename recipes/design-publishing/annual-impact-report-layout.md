# Annual Impact Report Layout

Design an evidence-traceable impact report with readable charts and clearly labeled missing information.

## Scenario

A small nonprofit has approved its annual figures and stories but has no consistent report design. Some metrics use different definitions across years. You need an attractive, readable report that preserves those caveats and makes it easy for the board to review every claim before public release.

## Inputs to prepare

- Approved narrative, metric table, definitions, and source references
- Intended audience, reporting period, and planned visibility
- Authorized photographs and consent-approved stories
- Page budget, brand guidance, and required financial or methodology notes

## Copy this prompt into dot

```text
dot, design an annual impact report for [ORGANIZATION] covering [REPORTING PERIOD] using [APPROVED NARRATIVE, METRICS, AND SOURCES]. The intended readers and visibility are [AUDIENCE AND SCOPE]. Begin with a claim-to-source table and flag missing definitions, inconsistent year comparisons, unapproved personal stories, or totals that do not reconcile. Do not invent beneficiary quotes, achievements, audit status, or financial figures.

Propose a [PAGE BUDGET]-page structure with a clear opening summary, evidence-led sections, readable charts, and visible methodology notes. Keep chart labels and narrative figures tied to the supplied table. Use [AUTHORIZED PHOTOGRAPHS] only within their approved purpose; exclude identifying or sensitive details not approved for this report. Explain visual choices simply so first-time reviewers can comment on them.

If suitable tools are available, deliver editable source, a review PDF, chart data notes, and a publication checklist. Otherwise provide the complete content-and-layout specification. Verify totals, units, reporting dates, chart baselines, captions, and a page containing an unusually long footnote. Show missing data explicitly rather than smoothing it away. Test screen reading order and print legibility where possible and disclose limitations. Keep the report private until I approve the final claims, personal-data use, files, and exact publication audience or destination.
```

## Iterate with a purpose

### 1. Reconcile evidence

```text
Recheck every headline number and comparison against the supplied metric table, listing unresolved discrepancies for the report owner.
```

### 2. Review an executive summary

```text
Prepare a one-page summary using only verified report claims and retaining the most important definition and comparison caveats.
```

### 3. Prepare the release checklist

```text
Create a final approval checklist covering sources, story consent, chart labels, export accessibility, and the named publication destination without publishing.
```

## Expected deliverables

- Report structure and designed review draft
- Editable source or complete layout specification
- Claim-to-source and chart-data notes
- Unresolved-data register
- Publication approval checklist

## Acceptance checks

- Every quantitative headline maps to a supplied source
- Chart units and reporting periods are visible
- Changed definitions are disclosed near comparisons
- Missing data is distinct from zero
- A long footnote remains legible and attached to its claim
- No unapproved quote, story, or audit claim appears

## Access, privacy and stop conditions

- The design does not audit or certify organizational figures
- Document, chart, and accessibility exports depend on available tools
- Personal stories and photos need approval for the specific audience
- Publication and third-party uploads are separate approval steps

## Two possible extensions

- Create a board-review slide summary from approved claims
- Design a reusable metric collection sheet for next year
