# Room Palette Preview Board

Explore room color combinations through labeled visual previews and a practical sample-testing plan.

## Scenario

You have authorized photos of a small sitting room and want to compare wall colors before buying sample paint. Daylight changes throughout the day, and some furniture must stay. You need visual direction that makes uncertainty about real paint appearance clear.

## Inputs to prepare

- Authorized room photos, ideally under two lighting conditions
- Surfaces that may change and furniture that must stay
- Three palette ideas or color references
- The desired mood, approximate budget, and intended private review audience

## Copy this prompt into dot

```text
dot, help me compare color directions for [ROOM] using [AUTHORIZED ROOM PHOTOS]. Only change [PERMITTED SURFACES]; keep [FURNITURE AND FIXED FEATURES] recognizable. Build three palette concepts from [COLOR REFERENCES] that support [DESIRED MOOD]. First identify shadows, mirrors, windows, and mixed lighting that could make the previews misleading.

If image editing is available, create comparable views with the same camera framing and label each candidate outside the edited image where possible. Pair each view with a plain palette swatch sheet and a short explanation of the intended contrast between walls, trim, and furnishings. Avoid adding structural features or suggesting that furniture dimensions were measured from the photograph. Note any objects or edges altered unintentionally by the tool.

Provide a comparison board, a list of unresolved lighting issues, and a small physical sample-testing plan. Check whether the three palettes remain distinguishable in both supplied lighting conditions and whether dark corners lose useful detail. Treat the renders as exploratory illustrations: screens, lighting, paint finish, and generative edits prevent reliable color matching. Do not buy paint, contact contractors, share room photographs, or publish the board. If tools cannot edit images here, deliver the annotated plan instead.
```

## Iterate with a purpose

### 1. Compare lighting

```text
Compare the selected palette under the two supplied lighting conditions and identify walls that need larger physical samples.
```

### 2. Add accent options

```text
Propose two reversible accent changes using only [AUTHORIZED FURNISHING CATEGORIES], with separate previews rather than silently changing the room.
```

### 3. Prepare sample record

```text
Create a printable observation sheet for recording sample color, finish, time of day, and what the user sees in the actual room.
```

## Expected deliverables

- Three labeled room palette previews if supported
- Palette swatch and comparison board
- Lighting and edit-limitations notes
- Physical paint-sample testing plan

## Acceptance checks

- Only permitted surfaces are intentionally changed
- All concepts use comparable framing
- Mirrors and window reflections are checked for inconsistent edits
- A dark corner or mixed-light area is explicitly reviewed
- Color labels are presented separately if image text is unreliable
- No exact paint match or measurement accuracy is claimed

## Access, privacy and stop conditions

- Photo recoloring depends on available image tools
- Rendered colors are not reliable paint or material samples
- Private room images are not shared externally without approval
- The project provides visual exploration, not structural or electrical advice

## Two possible extensions

- Track observations from actual sample patches
- Prepare a separate furniture-layout brief using verified dimensions
