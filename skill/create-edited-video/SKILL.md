# Create an edited video

Deliver the requested video, not just a storyboard, edit plan or export recipe. The sequence should work for its audience and purpose, whether it is a brief demonstration, a family montage, an interview excerpt or a longer explanation. Keep planning and handoff proportional to the edit.

## Establish what the edit must accomplish

Use the brief and available material to identify the intended viewer, purpose, required content, approximate or hard duration, viewing shape, tone and deliverables. Distinguish a strict limit from a preference: a runtime target does not justify cutting a necessary qualification or making a demonstration misleading. Honor an existing script, edit, brand treatment or chosen application when supplied.

Ask only for missing choices that materially change the result or block it. For reversible choices, make a reasonable draft decision and identify it briefly. Do not assume every video needs a promotional hook, music, a logo, narration or a call to action.

Inspect the accessible sources and editing/rendering tools before choosing an approach. Use the user's existing project when appropriate; otherwise use an available editor or rendering workflow capable of the requested result. An edit request does not imply a new application installation, stock purchase, recording session, upload to an outside service or publication. Use supplied or authorized assets; do not treat a publicly viewable clip or song as permission to reuse it.

If an essential source or capability is unavailable, identify the specific gap and complete useful preparation that does not depend on it. Do not substitute unrelated footage, an invented transcript or a plan and call the video finished. If the requested editable format cannot be produced, resolve that delivery choice rather than silently providing a flattened video alone.

## Choose material that supports the sequence

Review enough of each candidate source to understand what precedes and follows the selected moment. Use transcripts, thumbnails and metadata to find candidates, then inspect the picture and sound with available capabilities. Clearly distinguish actual media review from source descriptions or supplied timing.

Select for a role in the sequence: orientation, action, explanation, evidence, reaction or resolution. Remove redundant coverage before compressing everything into fragments. For instructions, preserve the steps and outcomes a viewer needs; for a montage, prioritize variety and emotional or visual progression without forcing a factual chronology that the sources do not support.

Keep the meaning of speech and events intact. Do not splice separated words into a new statement, remove a qualification that changes a claim, or place a reaction so it appears to answer a different event. When compressing time or combining different occasions could mislead, retain context or label the transition. Illustrative footage should not appear to prove something it does not depict.

Preserve originals. Keep source in/out points and their output positions in the timeline or a compact edit record when needed to revise the cut, rebuild it or align captions. Inspect rotation and framing before choosing crops; protect faces, action, diagrams and text that carry the meaning. Mixed source shapes can be fitted or deliberately reframed without stretching the picture.

## Assemble and refine the actual cut

Build a playable rough cut early. Establish a comprehensible opening, an intentional progression and a resolved ending, with the amount of context the purpose warrants. Adjust selection and order before spending time on decorative effects.

- Trim speech at natural thought boundaries and action where the viewer can follow it. Remove dead time while preserving necessary pauses, breathing room and visual proof of a result. Do not impose one shot length on every type of material
- Judge pacing against the content: a chart, instruction or unfamiliar name may need more screen time than a familiar establishing image. If the runtime is too long, reduce repetition or scope before making all text and speech faster
- Use cuts and transitions to express a relationship. Check position, movement and audio across adjacent shots; an intentional discontinuity is different from an accidental jump or a cut that hides a required step
- Keep speech intelligible relative to music and effects. Check abrupt level changes, clipped words, unintended silence and audible cut boundaries. Use only authorized music or narration, and preserve meaningful source sound when it helps explain what is happening
- Make titles and labels readable in the intended viewing shape, with enough time to read them. Check their facts against the source and their placement against important picture content. Do not add claims merely to make the edit feel complete

Keep picture, sound, overlays and any captions on the same final timeline. Recheck downstream timing after cuts, speed changes or inserted sections. Caption authoring and synchronization belong to `caption-delivery-pass` when that guide is available and needed; provide the final edit or a reliable mapping to it. Do not reuse source captions unchanged after rearranging the footage.

## Export, inspect and deliver

Save a distinct output in the requested format and destination. Export problems or a documented target compatibility requirement may warrant `media-export-compatibility` when available; this guide owns the editorial result, not a duplicate codec checklist. Do not upload or publish merely to demonstrate that export succeeded.

Review the exported file, not only the editing timeline. When playback is available, watch and listen through a short deliverable; for longer work, cover the sequence and concentrate additional checks on cuts, altered timing, dense text and uncertain source material. State any unreviewed intervals. Frame inspection and technical checks can find defects but do not establish that playback was watched or audio was heard.

Check that the final file contains the intended sequence and ending, meets the requested runtime and viewing shape, opens or decodes with an available tool, and has the expected sound and text. Look for blank gaps, unintended repeated frames or sections, missing media, broken overlays, lost context and drift between sound and picture. Correct material defects and recheck the affected passages in the new export.

If an editable project was requested, save it with the assets or references needed to reopen it, subject to the authorized delivery scope. Check for missing dependencies when reopening is available. Identify required application/version or external assets that affect usability; a rendered video or shot list is not an editable project.

Lead the handoff with the saved video and requested project. Briefly state the duration, meaningful editorial choices and any unresolved limits. Describe only the checks actually performed. Stop when the requested artifacts are usable and reviewed to the stated extent, or explain the precise missing source, decision or access that prevents completion.
