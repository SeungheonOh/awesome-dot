# Assemble a Warranty Claim the Evidence Can Support

Prepare a coherent request that lets the provider evaluate the claim without chasing avoidable omissions. Keep three conclusions separate: what the warranty documents say, whether the packet is complete, and whether this item is actually eligible.

## When to use

Use when someone needs help preparing or making a warranty claim for a purchased item. Focus on claim evidence, completeness and an accurate request. A return-window calculation alone belongs to a return-deadline workflow. This skill does not diagnose a fault, guarantee coverage, determine statutory rights or settle a dispute.

Use the user's actual authorized documents and evidence. Fictional fixtures are for tests only. Do not substitute a sample receipt, generated product image or invented conversation when real evidence is missing.

## Intake and scope

Establish the item/model, purchase and delivery records, seller, manufacturer or warranty provider, country/channel, applicable warranty document, assessment date, reported symptoms, desired remedy and existing case number if any. Ask for missing facts only when they affect the packet or next step; complete useful extraction while waiting.

Identify the intended action precisely: private preparation, clarification to a named recipient, or submission to a specific provider through a verified channel. Record which purchase details, product identifiers and attachments the user authorized sharing. A request to summarize coverage does not authorize contact. A request to submit a routine claim with specified evidence can authorize that submission; do not require a redundant approval when all required scope and approvals are already established.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the source record without changing it

Read the authorized receipt/order record, delivery evidence, warranty terms, product label, symptom notes and any prior support exchange. Prefer a connected source when it supports the task. Verify a provider's submission route through its official site or the already-authenticated purchase record; a contact address in an unverified message is insufficient.

Give each source an evidence ID, filename or exact link, relevant page/section, date and provenance. Preserve original files and record a checksum when working with local attachments. Distinguish original evidence, transcriptions and derived/redacted copies. Verify important OCR text against the page, especially dates, model identifiers, exclusions and negations. If originals disagree, preserve the disagreement and ask the smallest resolving question.

A product photo establishes what is visible, not the cause of a fault. Never fabricate a receipt, alter a defect photo to strengthen a claim, recreate a serial label or relabel a synthetic image as evidence. Use marked redactions or format conversions only for the authorized purpose and keep the original intact. For a shareable derivative, inspect rendered pages and underlying text/metadata so unrelated personal or payment information does not remain recoverable.

### 2. Map the actual terms to this purchase

Read purchase-specific terms first. If additional terms are needed, use the official provider source and record retrieval date, region, product scope and version. A current general policy may not govern an older purchase; keep both visible when applicability is unresolved.

Extract a coverage matrix with:

- Eligible purchaser, seller/channel, territory and product category
- Coverage start event and duration, with the source's counting convention
- Covered faults, exclusions and evidence the provider requires
- Available remedies and who chooses them
- Claim route, inspection requirements, shipping instructions, charges and any stated conditions

For each row distinguish documented rule, case evidence and conclusion: supported, contradicted or unknown. Being within a stated time period proves only that date condition. “Bought from a retailer” does not prove authorized-retailer status. A user's account of no known impact damage is a reported history, not an independent inspection. Do not decide that an exclusion applies merely because a symptom could be consistent with it.

Calculate date conditions only when their inputs and interpretation are evidenced. Preserve unresolved trigger dates, month-end rules and conflicting documents. Do not convert a warranty summary into legal advice or promise enforceability, free service, a refund or acceptance.

### 3. Build an accurate symptom chronology

Capture when the issue was first noticed, what the user observed, frequency, ordinary use conditions, and any already-performed troubleshooting with its result. Attribute each statement: user-reported, visible in an attachment, or documented by the provider. Keep suspected causes in a separate, explicitly unconfirmed field, or omit them when they add no value.

Do not invent tests, claim that a reset worked, or call the product defective as an established diagnosis. Do not instruct the user to open equipment, bypass safety features or reproduce a potentially dangerous condition for evidence. If there are signs of immediate danger, pause testing and use official safety instructions or qualified help; the packet can document existing observations without further exposure.

### 4. Assemble the review packet

Prepare a short request and an attachment manifest, using only the relevant facts:

1. Item/model and the necessary order/serial identifiers
2. Purchase/delivery dates and seller with source references
3. Concise symptoms and chronology, including actual troubleshooting or “none reported”
4. Desired remedy framed as a request, conditional on coverage when eligibility is unresolved
5. Evidence manifest: source ID, purpose, original/derivative status and included/not included
6. Missing facts and a specific question about any eligibility ambiguity

Distinguish missing required material from optional evidence that might help. A complete application can still have unresolved coverage; an apparently covered item can still have an incomplete packet. Do not overstate either status. Keep unknown answers visible rather than guessing fields to satisfy a form. Ask the provider to assess eligibility when the permitted request accepts uncertainty; if a required checkbox demands an unsupported assertion, stop before submitting it.

Exclude unrelated purchases, full payment details, account exports, private household imagery and unnecessary identifying information. Do not include sensitive personal details merely to make the account more persuasive. Save the completed packet in the authorized private destination and reopen it to confirm source references, legibility, redactions and uncertainty survived export.

### 5. Submit only the specifically authorized routine request

If preparation alone was requested, stop with the packet and the exact remaining questions. If a specific routine submission is already authorized:

- Recheck recipient/channel, item, attachments and data against that authorization. Resolve missing mandatory fields from authorized evidence or the user; do not invent an answer or silently omit a material fact
- Inspect the final form or message. Identify any mandatory declarations, new terms, charges, paid diagnosis, nonrefundable fees, shipping purchase, liability waiver or other commitment
- Pause for any new charge, waiver, commitment, sensitive-data transmission or applicable approval not already covered. Do not check “accept paid repair,” authorize disposal or agree to a replacement settlement as part of a basic claim request
- Follow applicable login, credential, CAPTCHA and legal-agreement requirements. Never put credentials in the packet. A blocked channel does not authorize outreach to a different recipient or an expanded disclosure
- Submit once when the exact action is authorized and required approvals are satisfied. Capture the submitted content, attachment list, timestamp, case/reference number and confirmation. A saved draft or uploaded attachment is not proof of submission
- If the response is uncertain, inspect the existing case or confirmation before retrying. Do not create duplicate claims. Read back the visible case details when supported and report any discrepancy

State “submitted” only with confirmation, and “accepted for coverage” only with an actual provider decision. Receipt of a case number is not a warranty approval. If the next step involves shipping, repair charges, accepting a remedy or closing a claim, treat it as a separate action under the user's instructions and applicable approvals.

## Finished output and verification

Deliver the request, evidence manifest, coverage matrix, completeness result and unresolved questions. If a submission occurred, include its verified status and provider reference; otherwise label it prepared, incomplete or awaiting an identified approval.

Before delivery:

- Check every factual claim against a source and every reported observation against its attribution
- Reconcile item/model/serial, seller, dates and applicable terms across the packet
- Confirm original evidence hashes remain unchanged and derivatives are clearly labeled
- Check required evidence versus available attachments and keep eligibility separate from completeness
- Verify that no speculative diagnosis, fabricated proof, unapproved charge or commitment entered the request
- For an external action, verify the exact authorized recipient/data and confirmation rather than assuming success

The [fictional packet](example.md) demonstrates a complete written-review packet whose eligibility is still unresolved. Run `python3 check_example.py` to check the date boundary, completeness distinction, evidence integrity, private export/readback and submission-scope guardrails. The fixture does not exercise a live provider.

## Example request

“Use my purchase confirmation, delivery record, warranty document and symptom notes to prepare a claim for this task lamp. Say what is documented and what the provider still needs to decide. Keep the originals intact and make a private packet for review. Do not submit it yet.”
