# Fictional intake pilot source packet

Every organization, observation and proposal in this packet is invented for this example. These are the complete approved inputs, not a report about a real team. Packet version: 1, dated 2026-10-01.

<a id="s0-brief"></a>
## S0. Approved presentation brief

Audience: operations leaders who can approve an extension and assign existing staff coverage. They know the intake queue but have not seen the measurement details. Purpose: a live decision discussion, with speaker notes for later reading. Deliver exactly five slides, including the decision slide, in editable form. Use a simple light background, dark text and one blue evidence chart. Do not add a separate cover or decorative photographs.

Proposed recommendation: extend the intake pilot for four workweeks, October 5–30, 2026, with a review on November 6, 2026. The request is for this presentation only. The extension, staffing allocation and proposed review rules remain unapproved. Do not send the deck or change operations.

<a id="s1-measurement-notes"></a>
## S1. Measurement notes

The fictional Cedar Operations team tested a single intake checklist in one queue. A request is the counting unit, not a person. The comparison covers completed requests only, using elapsed hours from arrival to first completed response.

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completion window, 2026 | September 7–11 | September 14–18 |
| Completed requests | 40 | 60 |
| Median elapsed time to first completed response | 18 hours | 12 hours |
| Requests reopened within seven calendar days | 4 | 7 |

All completed requests have a full seven-day reopening observation window, ending no later than September 25. The medians are supplied summary statistics. The packet contains no individual durations, so a reader cannot independently reconstruct them. The reopening fractions can be calculated from the supplied counts.

<a id="s2-interpretation-limits"></a>
## S2. Interpretation limits

The same team handled the same queue in two consecutive, nonrandom weeks. Workload mix and individual case complexity were not recorded. The packet does not count abandoned or still-open requests. There is no control group, satisfaction survey, staff-time log or cost measurement.

These figures describe the observed completed requests. They do not establish that the checklist caused the difference, that quality stayed unchanged, that all requests got faster or that the pilot saved labor or money. No significance test or uncertainty interval has been supplied.

<a id="s3-extension-proposal"></a>
## S3. Extension proposal

The operations lead proposes the October 5–30 extension in the same queue with the same checklist. Proposed coverage is at most eight staff-hours per week, reassigned from existing coverage. There is no request for additional spending. Reassigning coverage has an opportunity cost that the packet does not quantify. Record actual staff time if the extension is approved.

The operations lead would own the extension and record volume, completion times, reopening counts, open/abandoned requests and staff-hours. The quality lead would review reopened cases. Roles are proposed, not accepted commitments. The November 6 review allows seven calendar days of follow-up for requests completed on October 30.

<a id="s4-proposed-review-rules"></a>
## S4. Proposed review rules

The sponsor proposes these rules for leaders to discuss and approve. They are decision criteria, not observed results or validated statistical thresholds:

- Review median response time against a proposed target of at most 12 hours for completed requests
- Review the seven-day reopening rate against a proposed ceiling of 12%, showing both reopened and completed request counts
- Review actual staff time against the proposed cap of eight staff-hours per week
- Review all logged serious service failures individually; any such failure triggers an immediate pause and leadership review

Do not expand beyond the one queue without a separate decision. A result within these proposed thresholds does not prove causal benefit or quality equivalence. At the November 6 review, leaders choose whether to stop, revise or consider a separately approved expansion. Small denominators and request mix must remain visible.

<a id="s5-rejected-draft-claims"></a>
## S5. Claims from an unapproved draft

An earlier fictional draft says: “The checklist made every request 33% faster, quality was unchanged, and the team saved eight hours each week.” None of those claims is approved. Do not repeat them as findings. The 18-to-12-hour medians support a six-hour observed difference in the two completion samples. Eight staff-hours is a proposed coverage cap, not measured savings.
