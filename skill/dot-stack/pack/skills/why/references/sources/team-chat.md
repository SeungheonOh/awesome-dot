# Team discussion records

## Useful evidence

Contemporaneous threads can preserve alternatives, incident decisions, reviewer questions, constraints, and post-merge discoveries that never reached a formal document. Chat is informal and sometimes ephemeral; a casual suggestion is not automatically an adopted decision.

## Search and read context

Inspect the actual connected search and thread-fetch tools. Use an observed author identity, project/channel, and a date window around the target decision when those filters are supported. Start with linked review URLs, feature names, symbols, and exact error strings; try prior or colloquial names if the initial query misses.

Use verified channels and source identifiers, not guessed names. Read the relevant full thread, including later corrections and linked decisions. A message quoted elsewhere may omit the reply that rejected it. Do not search unrelated direct messages or private spaces to make the answer feel complete.

If authentication or access fails, report that source unavailable. Do not change permissions or switch to another transport to evade the denial. A successful search returning no matches is a narrower, different result.

## Weigh the record

An explicit dated decision from the responsible people is useful direct evidence of what was decided. A joke, tentative suggestion, reaction, or summary by someone uninvolved may only be a lead. Attribute claims accurately without treating job title or confidence as proof.

Check retention limits and edit history where exposed. Missing early messages can make a thread incomplete. Do not claim a decision never occurred merely because it is absent from searchable history. Cross-check links to the corresponding review or document when it changes the answer.

## Return

Provide exact message/thread permalink or supported ID, relevant channel, participants where useful, dates, short attributed statements, and context. Distinguish proposal, agreement, rejection, and later revision. Record queries, time range, unsearched private channels, and retention/access gaps. Do not post a follow-up question or share the findings externally as part of research.
