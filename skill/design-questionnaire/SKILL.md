# Design a Questionnaire

Deliver the actual blank instrument people can answer and the definitions needed to use its answers. A list of research goals or advice about better questions is not the requested questionnaire. Match the requested form: a short response can contain the complete questions; an editable file or native form needs the actual saved artifact.

Use this for self-completed research questions and operational intake. Moderated interviews and usability sessions belong to research preparation. If the questions and behavior are already decided, document formatting or interface implementation can produce the requested format without redesigning the instrument. Filling a supplied form and analyzing received answers have different primary workflows.

## Start from the use of an answer

Read the goals, service facts, existing questions and any supplied constraints. Establish who can answer, what one submission describes, and what decision or next action the answers should support. A person, a recent attempt and each item in a report are different units. Keep repeated items separate when they can have different answers or follow-up needs.

For each proposed question, identify the useful distinction its answer makes. Remove questions that have no relevant use; do not collect names, contact details or background attributes merely because similar forms do. Separate information needed to route the instrument from information used in the final decision. Ask about an unresolved policy when it would change an answer's meaning or permitted use; make ordinary reversible wording and layout choices within the request.

Make the reference clear where it matters: which item, episode, period, version or perspective the person should use. If the task concerns a recent experience, give a truthful route to people who have not had that experience or cannot remember it. Do not make them rate something they did not use. Keep retrospective experience, current conditions, future preference and anticipated behavior distinguishable; a stated preference does not establish what someone will do.

Preserve established wording or measures when the user needs comparability, and record a material change rather than quietly treating it as the same question. A familiar-looking scale is not evidence of a validated measure. Do not invent respondent findings, population coverage or a sampling design to justify the questions.

## Design the question and answer together

Write wording a person can understand without a moderator supplying missing context. Ask one interpretable thing at a time. Separate two dimensions when a single answer could conceal opposing responses, such as finding information and understanding it. Avoid embedding the preferred answer, an unsupported premise or a presumed cause in the question. Use brief local instructions where the meaning genuinely needs them.

Choose the answer form for its intended use:

- A single choice needs alternatives that do not overlap for valid respondents. State interval boundaries when ranges meet. Include an appropriate way to answer outside the listed categories rather than silently excluding legitimate cases
- Multiple choices need a clear selection instruction and coherent combinations. Decide how exclusive answers such as none or cannot recall relate to ordinary selections; an empty selection is not automatically an explicit none
- A rating needs a clear object, direction and interpretable labels. Keep comparable scales consistent where that helps the person answer. Do not assume ordinal steps have equal numerical distance or force every task onto a rating scale
- A number, date or time needs relevant units, precision and reference. Permit approximate or unknown answers when the purpose does not require an exact value. Do not make someone invent an identifier, timestamp or quantity merely to pass a required field
- Open text is useful when the person's wording matters or the categories are unsettled. Keep the prompt focused, make optional detail optional, and avoid requesting private material that the task does not need

Keep meaningful absence states distinct. Unanswered, intentionally skipped, not applicable, unknown, explicit none and known zero may support different conclusions. Use only the distinctions this instrument needs, but do not collapse them through blank cells or convenient defaults. State answer meanings in a compact dictionary when the user requests it or a downstream reader would otherwise misinterpret them; a simple instrument does not require a separate schema package.

In operational intake, distinguish what the person observed from what they suspect. Collect enough information for the next authorized step without demanding a diagnosis or an unrequested test. Requiredness should follow that purpose, including people with missing labels or incomplete recall.

## Make the complete route answerable

Order questions around the respondent's task and knowledge. Explain the purpose in the instrument and keep the ordinary route concise. Put prerequisites before dependent questions, and ensure valid participants can reach an appropriate finish without inventing an answer. A short questionnaire should not acquire a mandatory intake interview or an elaborate section hierarchy.

For paper or plain text, make skip instructions explicit and unambiguous. For conditional forms, define when a field is shown, when it is required and how its answer is interpreted. Check that hidden fields cannot remain impossible requirements. Distinguish one repeated item's answers from another item's answers; equal names do not necessarily identify the same item.

Where earlier answers can change, decide what happens to dependent answers. A retained draft can be useful, but inactive values must not silently become current answers. Specify whether they are cleared, preserved separately or offered for review, and how that affects the current export. Treat this as a design proposal when the service has not established a rule. Do not claim that a prototype implements it unless the actual behavior was exercised.

Keep completion wording faithful to the actual stage. Finishing a local preview, saving a draft, submitting a response and receiving an acknowledgement are different events. Do not invent a service deadline, receipt, contact channel or anonymity guarantee. If collection is requested, use the authorized supported destination and its actual capabilities; designing the instrument alone does not imply distribution or response collection.

## Produce and inspect the requested instrument

Author the complete respondent-facing material in the requested format. Keep internal coding and analysis notes out of the respondent's path unless they help the person answer. Use native controls and supported labels when a functioning form is requested; a static specification or drawing is useful only when described as that artifact. Follow the selected document or application workflow for editing, rendering and runtime checks.

Check the saved instrument together with its answer definitions and routing, rather than reviewing each in isolation. Walk a few representative paths chosen for consequential distinctions: the ordinary respondent, a valid nonuser or unknown answer, a repeated item, or a changed branch when relevant. Use clearly fictional answers for preparation checks and retain the blank deliverable. Confirm that each path asks answerable questions, reaches the intended finish and produces the intended current answer meaning.

For implemented controls or exports, exercise the real saved consumer and inspect actual values, including inactive fields and repeated entries where relevant. For a design-only request, walk the specified behavior and label those results as design checks. Reopen saved files, verify wording and option consistency, and inspect the visible layout. Do not infer keyboard behavior, assistive-technology compatibility or successful submission from source markup or a screenshot.

Author review and fictional walkthroughs can reveal contradictions; they do not demonstrate how real respondents interpret, recall or answer a question. When actual testing is authorized and available, examine those interpretation steps as well as completion behavior, then revise what the evidence supports. Do not claim a completion time from a proposed target or a burden estimate as if it were observed. Keep version changes visible if answers will later be compared.

## Return the usable result

Lead with the actual questionnaire or verified artifact. Include only the supporting definitions, material design choices and check limits needed to use it correctly or explicitly requested by the user. A short blank-question request can end with the questions; do not attach an automatic research protocol, coding ledger or implementation packet.

State whether the result is a reviewable instrument, an implemented private form or an activated collection route, according to what was actually done. Keep unresolved questions and untested respondent interpretation visible without presenting invented findings or a universal quality certificate. If a specific platform step is blocked, deliver the completed material and identify the missing capability precisely.
