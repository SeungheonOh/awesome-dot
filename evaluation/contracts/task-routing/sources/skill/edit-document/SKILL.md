# Edit a document

Produce the revised document the user asked for. Preserve its supported meaning and the author's voice while improving what the intended reader needs to understand or do. A list of suggestions is not a substitute for an edit unless the user requested review only.

## Set the editorial scope

Use the request, the draft, and any supplied style guide or reference to establish the audience, purpose, desired tone, length, and degree of change. Identify constraints such as required headings, a template, quoted language, terminology, or sections outside the assignment.

Ask only when the answer changes a material editorial choice: which draft to edit, whether the audience is internal or external when confidential context matters, or which of two conflicting statements is correct. Infer routine choices from the document. For an unqualified “clean this up,” improve correctness and clarity without replacing the author's stance or rebuilding the document. If a question blocks one passage, complete independent edits and keep that passage's issue visible.

Match the depth to the request rather than treating every edit as a rewrite:

- **Proofreading:** Correct spelling, grammar, punctuation, and unambiguous mechanical inconsistencies. Preserve argument, voice, order, and wording that already works. Flag ambiguities that cannot be corrected without choosing a meaning.
- **Tightening or substantive rewriting:** Improve sentences, transitions, emphasis, and accessibility for the stated reader. Remove repetition, explain terms using supplied context, and recast awkward passages. Preserve the author's claims, qualifications, and intended commitments.
- **Restructuring:** Reorder, combine, split, or retitle sections when the user requests it or clearly asks for a different reading sequence. Organize around the reader's task or decision; retain required template structure and keep supporting detail attached to the point it supports.

These are degrees of intervention, not separate mandatory passes. A request may combine them or set different scopes for different sections.

## Read before rewriting

Read the complete draft before editing its opening in isolation. Understand the conclusion, how sections depend on each other, what the audience already knows, and what the author is trying to achieve. Include relevant tables, captions, footnotes, links, and comments; extracted body text may omit material that changes a sentence's meaning.

For a long document, read in coherent sections and retain a brief internal map of its argument, terminology, repeated facts, and dependencies. If some content is inaccessible, state the coverage limit and do not present a partial edit as a whole-document review.

Distinguish facts and constraints from editorial choices. Watch especially for names, dates, quantities and units, negation, conditions, attribution, causal claims, uncertainty, and the difference between a proposal and a commitment. Notice the author's characteristic level of formality, directness, vocabulary, and point of view. Keep useful individuality rather than replacing it with generic polished prose.

## Make the edit

Work from the selected source in the requested form. Preserve native document elements needed by the user, including tables, headings, links, notes, and review markup. Use format-aware tools when necessary; do not rebuild a structured document as plain text and silently discard those elements. For an in-place edit, inspect the current source before writing so intervening changes are not overwritten. When no in-place update is requested, return revised text or a revised copy appropriate to the input.

Resolve organization and emphasis before polishing individual sentences when restructuring is in scope. Put the purpose, conclusion, or requested action where the reader needs it. Remove redundant material without losing a distinct reason, exception, counterargument, or necessary context. Tighten explanations to the reader's knowledge rather than stripping away every qualification or making every paragraph the same length.

Preserve meaning through the transformation:

- Change factual content when the user supplies the correction or authorized evidence supports it. Do not invent statistics, examples, quotations, citations, credentials, opinions, or commitments to make a passage more persuasive.
- Keep uncertainty and conditions attached to the claims they limit. “May,” “estimated,” “subject to,” and similar wording can carry substantive meaning.
- Retain citations and source attribution with the claims they support when moving or rewriting text. Do not expand a claim beyond its evidence or imply that an editorial pass independently verified a source.
- Keep exact quotations exact. If a paraphrase is appropriate, distinguish it from quoted wording and preserve its attribution.
- Resolve stylistic inconsistency consistently, but do not choose between contradictory facts just to smooth the prose. Keep the supported wording and make the precise unresolved point visible. When the conflict is part of the document's meaning or requested purpose, retain it in the text; otherwise flag the editorial question separately or through the requested comment mechanism.

If the requested length or format would force removal of essential meaning, make the best supported revision and explain the specific tradeoff. Do not fill missing evidence with plausible prose or conceal a substantive gap in a fluent sentence.

## Review in proportion to the change

Read the revised document as the intended audience, then compare it with the source. Check that the main point, supporting logic, stance, and requested action remain faithful to the assignment. Look for accidental changes to facts, obligations, qualifications, or relationships between table cells and surrounding prose.

For a proofread, inspect the corrections in context and stop when the requested defects are fixed. For a substantive rewrite, review changed claims and transitions alongside the original. For restructuring, also account for moved or removed content, update headings and cross-references, and check that definitions precede their use and sections still connect.

When editing a file or native document, reopen the saved result and confirm the intended changes and necessary structure survived. Inspect affected layout where text changes can disrupt tables, captions, or pagination. Use a separate export-fidelity workflow only when the task also calls for conversion or delivery-format checking; editing one draft does not require a version-reconciliation ledger or an export audit.

## Return the revised document

Deliver the actual revised text, file, or verified document link in the requested format. Provide tracked changes, a clean copy, or comments when requested and supported; describe any fallback honestly.

Keep the handoff proportional. Small corrections need little explanation. A substantial edit benefits from a short account of the main structural or tonal choices and any decisions still needed. Identify unresolved issues by section or a short excerpt so the user can act on them. Do not burden the user with every routine correction or call an edited claim fact-checked unless that work was done.
